# Product Requirements Document: Angus Teeton — Cinematic Portfolio

## Intent & Goal
A premium cinematic editorial portfolio website for Angus Teeton, a videographer, editor and storyteller specialising in sports and brand content. It replaces his basic Google Sites portfolio with a site that makes his work look exceptional, plays videos directly on the site, and communicates his experience, credibility and international exposure. The core objective is that within moments of arriving, a potential client understands the quality of his work, the types of projects he produces, the organisations he has worked with, and how one production can become an entire content campaign. Phase 1 delivers the complete homepage with the cinematic visual identity, plus a dashboard-managed video system so videos can be added by pasting any YouTube link, and an admin page with per-clip analytics for Angus and the site owner. Later phases (full project case-study pages, client directory, full CMS) remain out of scope.

## Audience & Roles
Public visitors: potential clients — international sports brands, sports nutrition and performance brands, athletes, event organisers, organisations and creative agencies evaluating whether to hire Angus — plus collaborators and curious viewers; they see the public site only, with no login. Admin role (Angus and the site owner only): access behind the platform's built-in login to a full admin page where they manage video records and view analytics (clicks and per-clip view counts).

## Core Flows
*These flows must work end-to-end:*

1. Visitor lands on the homepage and immediately understands who Angus is and what he does via a full-viewport cinematic hero (video or clearly labelled placeholder background, headline "Stories worth experiencing.", supporting line, Explore Projects action, subtle scroll indicator)
2. Visitor scrolls through the Experience and Impact section where the four supplied statistics (100+ YouTube projects, 23.5 million views, 23 thousand comments, 1.8 million hours of retention) animate in with viewport-triggered number animations
3. Visitor browses the Selected Work showcase — an editorial, asymmetrical layout presenting the seven supplied projects, each with title, client, category, short description, Angus's role and production details where supplied: Sweat Elite (68 videos, 50,000 new subscribers, 8.6M views), The Running Channel (19 videos, 14.9M views, 84,000 new subscribers), TrailFam (3-min organisation overview for pitches to brands and donors, filmed over 2 days and edited in 2 days), Scholarbook (recruiting-services video for American universities advocating for Kenyan runners, delivered 2 days after filming), Adventure Race World Championships 2025 (final race film for the closing ceremony, 10 days of footage across multiple terrains, plus daily recap videos for days 1, 2 and 5 edited overnight and delivered the next day), Precision PF&H (education-led sports nutrition content), and Cloud Digital (art gallery, cafe, co-working space and production studio, 24 narrative pieces delivered per month)
4. Visitor browses the video library grid, powered by Video entity records managed from the dashboard: each record has a youtube_url field accepting any YouTube format (watch URL, youtu.be link, shorts link, or a bare 11-character ID) and the site extracts the video ID so every format behaves identically
5. Video cards show a thumbnail from the record's thumbnail_url if provided, otherwise automatically fetch YouTube's own preview image (img.youtube.com/vi/ID/maxresdefault.jpg)
6. Visitor clicks a video card and an in-site popup player opens embedding youtube-nocookie.com/embed/ID and starts playing immediately — the viewer stays on the site with no new tab; every click logs an analytics event and increments that clip's view count
7. If YouTube blocks embedding (private videos never embed; unlisted works; owner-disabled embedding cannot be overridden), the popup offers a prominent Watch on YouTube button as the backup path
8. The 19 supplied YouTube links (7 from the first batch plus 12 more, a mix of full videos and Shorts) seed the initial Video records, alongside the curated project showcase on the homepage
